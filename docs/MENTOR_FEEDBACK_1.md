ROLLOVER — Mentor Session Final Brief
Core idea
ROLLOVER is a gamified discovery marketplace for surplus and end-of-cycle retail inventory.

It is not a waste-disposal service and it is not meant to magically make bad products desirable.

The problem we solve is:

Good product. Wrong buyer.

Retailers may have usable inventory that is difficult to move because it is end-of-season, overstocked, discontinued, an unusual variant, under-discovered, or approaching the end of its commercial cycle.

ROLLOVER helps that inventory reach people who are more likely to actually want it.


How it works
Customer flow:

Choose what categories you are open to → choose a quiz topic → answer gamified MCQs → inventory is dynamically filtered → roll a personalised drop → reveal → claim → payment

The quiz is directly connected to live available stock.

Example:

“Who is your favourite MLP character?”

Each answer secretly maps to filters such as:

practical, bold, fashion, calm, colourful, etc.

Those filters apply directly to the currently available inventory.

The next question is generated based on what stock remains.

This means:

The inventory shapes the quiz, and the quiz finds the buyer.

We do not need fixed personality types.


B2C strategy
Consumers should see ROLLOVER as:

fun + discovery + personalisation + surprise + value

We are not primarily selling “sustainability” to the customer.

The experience should feel like a game / gacha system:

Play → Roll → Reveal

Discounts can be highly visible where retailers allow them:

20% OFF / 35% OFF / etc.

Retailers that do not want public markdowns can opt for protected pricing instead.

Potential future mode:

“Rolling for me” / “Rolling for someone else”

This lets the same system support gifting without building a separate gifting product.


B2B strategy
We pitch retailers very differently.

Their problem is:

They already paid to produce inventory that may eventually be heavily discounted, written off or disposed of.

ROLLOVER gives that inventory another demand channel.

Business flow:

Retailer provides eligible inventory → sets pricing/discount rules → ROLLOVER generates demand → customer purchases → retailer fulfils → ROLLOVER takes commission

We should not charge companies simply to “get rid of stock.”

The cleaner model is:

ROLLOVER earns when the retailer earns.

Primary revenue model for the pitch:

Commission on successful sales.


Retailer control
Retailers keep control over:

what inventory enters ROLLOVER, available quantity, selling price, whether discounts are allowed, how large the discount may be, and whether discounts are publicly shown.

Example:

Mass-market stock:

32% OFF

Premium / brand-protected stock:

PRIVATE DROP / LIMITED DROP

No public crossed-out price required.

This reduces concerns about brand dilution.


Who we should partner with
ROLLOVER should not accept any random unsold product from any company.

Best early partners are inventories where discovery is a major problem:

smaller/emerging brands, fashion, beauty, lifestyle, stationery, specialty food, electronics/accessories, sustainable brands, and selected surplus lines from larger retailers.

Good candidates include:

end-of-season items, overstock, discontinued lines, unusual variants, odd sizes, short-dated but safe products, and products from brands with weak discovery.

ROLLOVER is less useful when:

The product is simply bad and nobody wants it.

We should not claim AI can solve that.


Why users would use ROLLOVER instead of the original store
ROLLOVER changes discovery, not just price.

Normal clearance:

Here are hundreds of unwanted products. Browse them yourself.

ROLLOVER:

Tell us a little about yourself and we will find things you may never have searched for.

Cross-brand drops are especially useful.

Example:

lip tint + planner + candle + snack

Those products may come from different retailers.

The combination itself becomes something new that no single retailer normally offers.


Product responsibility
ROLLOVER should not become a warehouse or inspection company.

Retailers remain responsible for:

product condition, expiry/safety, legal compliance, inventory accuracy and fulfilment.

ROLLOVER only works with stock the retailer has marked as eligible for sale.

This keeps the business scalable.


Secondary B2B value
ROLLOVER can eventually give retailers aggregated information such as:

what people selected, rejected, revealed and purchased.

This may help companies understand what kinds of surplus inventory still have demand.

However, this is a secondary benefit.

Do not turn the pitch into an analytics SaaS product.

Primary B2B value remains:

Recover value from stranded inventory.


Growth / trend argument
Do not rely on:

“It will go viral.”

The product should already provide value through:

discounts / exclusive access + discovery + personalisation + surprise.

The gamified quizzes and shareable results are an additional acquisition loop.

Possible social result:

“I ROLLED: CHAOTIC GOOD — $82 VALUE FOR $54”

Useful for growth, but not required for the business model to work.


What we are building for the hackathon
Keep the MVP focused.

BUILD:

Customer-facing ROLLOVER web app with:

category choice → quiz topic → AI-generated MCQs → direct inventory filtering → roll → reveal → claim/mock checkout

Use the existing fake XLSX inventory.

Show retailer-controlled discounts in the demo.

DO NOT BUILD:

real payments, retailer dashboard, logistics system, warehouse system, authentication, detailed analytics platform, Secret Santa system, complex pricing engine, full ERP integration.

Those can be described as future operations.


Main pitch message
Retailers don't need another place to dump surplus. They need another reason for people to discover it.

ROLLOVER turns stranded inventory into a personalised, gamified shopping experience.

For consumers:

Find something you wouldn't have gone looking for.

For retailers:

Recover value from inventory you've already made.

For ROLLOVER:

We earn a commission when a match becomes a sale.
One sentence
ROLLOVER is a gamified surplus marketplace where AI-generated quizzes dynamically filter real retail inventory to match under-discovered products with people more likely to want them.
